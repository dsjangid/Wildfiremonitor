01 // THE CRISIS

Why Traditional Suppression Models Fail

Wildfire behavior exhibits extreme heavy-tailed dynamics. Standard machine learning models minimize mean squared error and collapse during severe meteorological shocks, leaving incident commanders defenseless.

01.01

Heavy-Tailed Outlier Skew

Over 90% of forest fires burn under 5 hectares, but the top 1% cause 95% of total acreage destruction. Standard regression penalizes mean fluctuations and treats catastrophic threats as noise to be smoothed away.

01.02

Nonlinear Thermal Couplings

When ambient vapor pressure deficit exceeds critical fuel moisture thresholds, forest fire velocity accelerates exponentially rather than linearly, outpacing conventional heuristic models.

01.03

Dispatch Saturation Bottlenecks

Emergency commanders must allocate exactly 25 tactical crews. Over-concentrating units creates severe spatial bottlenecking (>4 crews/cell) while leaving adjacent perimeter sectors vulnerable.

02 // DOMAIN PHYSICS

Thermodynamic Atmospheric Engineering

Raw meteorological telemetry is transformed through domain-physics atmospheric equations before feeding the Hurdle-Ensemble.

Tetens Vapor Pressure Deficit (VPD)

Quantifies the evaporative drying demand on living tree canopies by coupling saturated vapor pressure $e_s(T)$ with relative humidity:

e_s(T) = 0.61078 \cdot \exp\left(\frac{17.27 \cdot T}{T + 237.3}\right)
\text{VPD} = e_s(T) \cdot \left(1 - \frac{\text{RH}}{100}\right)

Elevated VPD draws moisture out of fine combustible foliage within hours, precipitating rapid crown ignition.

Canadian Buildup Index (BUI)

Integrates the Duff Moisture Code (DMC) and deep Drought Code (DC) to represent total combustible organic fuel mass:

\text{BUI} = \frac{0.8 \cdot \text{DMC} \cdot \text{DC}}{\text{DMC} + 0.4 \cdot \text{DC}}
\text{Spread Potential} = \frac{\text{ISI} \cdot \text{Wind}}{10}

Ensures extreme predictive sensitivity during severe summer drought conditions in Mediterranean forest ecosystems.

03 // MULTI-TASK ARCHITECTURE

The Tri-Model Hurdle-Ensemble

A balanced multi-paradigm pipeline designed to rank high-impact threats without overfitting to stochastic small fires.

Normalized Unified Prioritization Formula

Individual model outputs are standardized to $[0, 1]$ and synthesized through calibrated loss weighting:

\text{Score} = 0.50 \cdot \text{Norm}(S_{\text{Ridge}}) + 0.20 \cdot \text{Norm}(S_{\text{Huber}}) + 0.30 \cdot P(\text{Area} \ge P_{80})
MODEL 01

Ridge Regressor

GLOBAL TREND (α=20.0)

Maintains monotonic ranking across standard meteorological variations without learning spurious feature noise from small fires.

MODEL 02

Huber Regressor

OUTLIER IMMUNITY (ε=1.35)

Applies linear loss penalty to massive burn areas, preventing single 1,000+ hectare anomalies from distorting global weights.

MODEL 03

Tail Classifier

P(AREA ≥ P80) (C=0.05)

Directly computes the posterior probability that a fire will breach the 80th percentile threshold, maximizing top-25 recall.
